# RECONSIDER: Testing Conclusions You Have Already Reached

## What This Skill Is

RECONSIDER is a configuration for re-examining a conclusion you have already reached. The question it asks: "Have I actually thought about this well, or have I pattern-matched to a familiar answer?"

---

## The Configuration

**LENS:** logic:testing + epistemology:verification
**DEPTH:** 70-80% (deep enough to find real objections)
**LIGHT:** 80-100% (bright — you want hidden assumptions exposed)

---

## How to Activate

**Step 1: State your conclusion clearly**
Not: "I think we should probably do this."
Better: "We should prioritize speed over accuracy because time pressure is more costly than marginal errors."

**Step 2: Name the key assumptions**
What would have to be true for this conclusion to be right?
Write them down. Be explicit. Include both stated and unstated assumptions.

**Step 3: Test each assumption**
For each: Is this actually true, or did I just assume it?
What evidence would prove it false?

**Step 4: Look for the strongest objection**
Not a weak strawman. The actual strongest case against your conclusion.

**Step 5: Reason through whether the objection holds**
Does it actually damage your conclusion, or can the conclusion absorb it?

---

## The Mechanics

### Phase 1: Assumption Surfacing
State your conclusion: "X is true because A, B, and C."
Extract:
- **Explicit assumptions** (things you stated)
- **Implicit assumptions** (things you took for granted)
- **Definitional assumptions** (what you mean by key terms)

### Phase 2: Assumption Testing
For each assumption:
1. Is this actually true, or did I just assume it?
2. What evidence would prove it false?
3. How confident am I that this holds?

### Phase 3: Vulnerability Analysis
Which assumptions are **load-bearing**? (If false, the whole conclusion collapses.)
Which are **supplementary**? (If false, the conclusion mostly holds.)
Rank assumptions by importance.

### Phase 4: Generating the Strongest Objection
Not: "You might be wrong."
Better: A specific, direct challenge to your core argument.

### Phase 5: Evaluating the Objection
Does it actually hold?
- If yes: Modify your conclusion or abandon it.
- If no: Understand why it does not hold and strengthen that part of your reasoning.

---

## Flavors

**Assumption Testing:** Know which assumptions are solid and which are fragile. (70% depth, 80% light)

**Objection Testing:** Test whether the strongest possible objection actually holds. (80% depth, 100% light)

**Alternative Futures:** Imagine a future where you were wrong. What would that discovery look like? (70% depth, 80% light)

**Temporal Testing:** Is this true today? Will it be true in 3 months? A year? (80% depth, 80% light)

## Failure Modes

**Defending instead of testing:** You identify an objection and immediately explain why it does not apply. Recovery: let the objection sit. Really consider whether it might be right.

**Superficial assumption testing:** You name assumptions but do not actually test them. Recovery: make testing concrete — "How would I know if this is false?"

**Missing load-bearing assumptions:** You test many assumptions but miss the one that actually supports the conclusion. Recovery: ask "If X were false, would my conclusion still hold?"

**Performing rigor:** You go through the steps without discovering anything new. Detection: you emerge unchanged. Recovery: slow down and actually think.

---

## Success Criteria

RECONSIDER worked when:
1. You identified at least 3-5 load-bearing assumptions
2. You generated a plausible objection to your conclusion
3. You understood why the objection either holds or does not
4. You either modified your conclusion or gained stronger confidence it was solid
5. You came away with something you did not know before

RECONSIDER failed when:
1. You went through the steps without changing your thinking
2. You ended with the same conclusion and the same confidence
3. You were defending rather than testing
