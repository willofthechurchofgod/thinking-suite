# CHALLENGE: Testing a Position Against Strongest Objections

## What This Skill Is

CHALLENGE is a configuration for actively testing a position by generating the strongest possible objections to it and reasoning through whether those objections hold. This is adversarial reasoning — not bad faith, but preparation.

---

## The Configuration

**LENS:** logic:adversarial + rhetoric:defense
**DEPTH:** 80-100% (deep enough to find real vulnerabilities)
**LIGHT:** 100% (maximum — you want to see all complexity and every vulnerability)

---

## How to Activate

**Step 1: State your position clearly**
Not: "We should do this thing."
Better: "We should prioritize feature X over feature Y because it will produce greater user value and faster ROI."

**Step 2: Generate the strongest possible objection**
Not: "Someone might disagree."
Better: A specific argument that directly contradicts your main claim, based on reasonable assumptions.

**Step 3: Assess whether the objection holds**
Does it actually contradict your position?
Or can your position absorb the objection?

**Step 4: Defend your position against the objection**
If the objection holds: modify your position.
If it does not hold: explain exactly why it does not.

**Step 5: Generate the next strongest objection**
Keep going until no stronger objection emerges.

---

## The Mechanics

### Phase 1: Position Clarity
State your position with precision. Include:
- What you are claiming
- Why you are claiming it
- What evidence supports it
- What assumptions it rests on

### Phase 2: Objection Generation
What is the strongest possible objection?
Not a weak strawman. The actual strongest case against you.
Ask: "What would have to be true for the opposite position to be right?" Make that the objection.

### Phase 3: Objection Assessment
Does this objection actually damage your position?
- Does it challenge your core claim?
- Is it based on reasonable assumptions?
- Would accepting the objection require changing your position?

### Phase 4: Defense
Three options:
- **Defend:** Show the objection is wrong — explain why it does not hold
- **Modify:** Adjust your position to account for what the objection reveals
- **Concede:** Acknowledge the objection is stronger and change your position

### Phase 5: Next Objection
Once addressed, generate the second strongest objection. Continue until no stronger objection emerges.

---

## Flavors

**Direct Challenge:** Test whether a position holds against direct opposition. (90% depth, 100% light)

**Assumption Challenge:** Test the assumptions a position rests on. (80% depth, 100% light) — "Your position assumes X. What if X is false?"

**Consequence Challenge:** Test whether you are prepared for the consequences of your position. (80% depth, 90% light) — "If your position is right, what follows? Are you prepared for that?"

**Evidence Challenge:** Test whether the evidence for your position is as solid as you think. (80% depth, 100% light) — "The evidence you cite could also support the opposite position. Why does it support yours?"

## Connections to Other Skills

- **RECONSIDER → CHALLENGE:** Test your position internally first, then test it against external attack
- **CONSTRAIN → CHALLENGE:** Understand the boundaries of your position, then defend those boundaries
- **CHALLENGE → REVIEW:** Challenge multiple positions, then see which ones are most defensible

---

## Failure Modes

**Generating weak objections:** You generate objections that do not actually challenge your position. Recovery: ask "What would have to be true for the opposite to be right?" Make that the objection.

**Defending without listening:** You generate an objection and immediately defend without really considering it. Recovery: let the objection sit. Could this actually be right?

**Capitulating too easily:** An objection is strong, so you immediately abandon your position. Recovery: reason through whether it actually undermines your position, not just whether it sounds strong.

**Performing challenge:** You go through the steps without actually thinking through the objections. Recovery: slow down. Try to think of an objection that would actually convince you to change your position.

**Missing the real vulnerability:** You challenge on surface issues while missing the foundational problem. Recovery: ask "What is my position really resting on? What if that is wrong?"

---

## Success Criteria

CHALLENGE worked when:
1. You generated an objection that actually threatened your position
2. You had to think hard to defend against it (or change your position)
3. You came away with either stronger confidence or a modified version of your position
4. You can articulate exactly why the objection does or does not hold
5. You understand where your position is strong and where it is vulnerable

CHALLENGE failed when:
1. All objections were weak and easy to dismiss
2. You defended without really listening
3. You came away unchanged
4. You could not identify the real vulnerability in your position
