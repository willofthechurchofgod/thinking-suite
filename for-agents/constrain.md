# CONSTRAIN: Finding the Boundaries of an Idea

## What This Skill Is

CONSTRAIN is a configuration for understanding where an idea, principle, or conclusion applies — and where it breaks. It is about precision: making claims more useful by knowing exactly when to apply them.

---

## The Configuration

**LENS:** logic:boundary-testing + empiricism:edge-cases
**DEPTH:** 70-80% (deep enough to find real boundaries)
**LIGHT:** 90-100% (bright — you want to see hidden exceptions)

---

## How to Activate

**Step 1: State the claim clearly**
Not: "Efficiency matters."
Better: "When resources are scarce, prioritizing efficiency produces better outcomes than prioritizing flexibility."

**Step 2: Identify obvious boundary conditions**
When is this claim definitely true? When is it definitely false? What conditions matter?

**Step 3: Push the boundaries**
Imagine a situation slightly different from where you know the claim is true. Does it still hold? Keep pushing until you find the edge.

**Step 4: Characterize the boundary**
What exactly is the condition that determines whether the claim holds?
Is it a sharp edge or a gradual transition?

**Step 5: Test the boundary**
Imagine situations right at the boundary. Does the claim hold or break? Why?

---

## The Mechanics

### Phase 1: Domain of Validity
Where is this claim definitely true?
Be specific. What context, what conditions, what constraints produce the case where this clearly holds?

### Phase 2: Domain of Invalidity
Where is this claim definitely false?
Be specific about where it breaks.

### Phase 3: Boundary Exploration
What is the transition zone?
What condition determines whether you are in the "definitely true" domain or the "definitely false" domain?

### Phase 4: Precision of the Boundary
Is the boundary sharp (clear transition) or fuzzy (gradual change)?
Can you state it precisely, or is it a matter of degree?

### Phase 5: Testing the Boundary
What would happen if you applied the claim right at the boundary?
Would it still hold? Why or why not?

---

## Flavors

**Scope Boundary:** Find the scope where a principle applies. (70% depth, 90% light) — "This principle works for small teams. Where does it break for large teams?"

**Condition Boundary:** Find the specific conditions under which a claim holds. (80% depth, 100% light) — "This works when X. What happens when X changes?"

**Strength Boundary:** Understand how strong the principle is under different conditions. (70% depth, 80% light) — "This is definitely true under condition A. How true is it under condition B?"

**Trade-off Boundary:** Find where optimizing one thing requires sacrificing another. (80% depth, 100% light) — "Optimizing for X requires sacrificing Y. Where is that trade-off worth it?"

## Connections to Other Skills

- **RECONSIDER → CONSTRAIN:** Test a conclusion, then find its limits
- **CONSTRAIN → CHALLENGE:** Understand boundaries, then defend them against attack
- **CONSTRAIN → REVIEW:** Find patterns across boundaries of multiple ideas

---

## Failure Modes

**False boundaries:** You identify a boundary that does not actually exist. Recovery: test the boundary hard. Push on it.

**Missing the real boundary:** You find a boundary but miss the one that actually matters. Recovery: ask "What condition really determines whether this is true?"

**Over-specification:** You make the boundary so specific it no longer applies to anything. Recovery: find the simplest characterization that still holds.

**Treating fuzzy boundaries as sharp:** Some boundaries are gradual. Recovery: characterize the transition zone, not just the edge.

**Ignoring interaction effects:** Multiple conditions interact. Recovery: test boundaries against multiple different contexts.

---

## Success Criteria

CONSTRAIN worked when:
1. You identified clear domains where the claim is true and false
2. You found the transition condition
3. You understand whether the boundary is sharp or fuzzy
4. You tested the boundary against multiple contexts
5. You came away able to state the claim much more precisely

CONSTRAIN failed when:
1. You could not identify the boundaries
2. The claim still seems to apply everywhere (that usually means you missed boundaries)
3. You could not characterize what condition determines the boundary
