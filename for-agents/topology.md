# TOPOLOGY: The Three-Dimensional Map of the Reasoning System

## What This Skill Is

Topology holds the complete shape of the reasoning system in three dimensions. It is consulted when the conductor needs more than the layered terrain map — when routing is ambiguous, when coupling is being considered, when the system needs to understand its own structure to navigate correctly.

Every other skill does something to a problem. Topology describes the shape of the space those skills occupy. It does not route. It does not direct. It returns a position and a description of what is nearby. The conductor uses that to decide what happens next.

---

## The Three Axes

### Axis 1: Time
*Where in the thinking process does this skill typically activate?*

This axis runs from earliest to latest in the natural flow of thinking.

It is not a strict sequence — skills can activate at any point if the problem demands it. But each skill has a natural position: the moment in the thinking process where it is most likely to be called and most likely to produce useful output.

**Early** — activated before the problem is understood. These skills work on the problem structure itself, before any analysis of the problem's content begins.

**Middle** — activated once the problem is clear and material is being generated or tested. These skills work on problem content directly.

**Late** — activated once substantial thinking has been done and the work is consolidating. These skills work on the outputs of other thinking.

**Throughout** — not positioned on the time axis because they are present at every stage. These are meta-skills: they direct and shape thinking at any moment rather than activating for a particular phase.

### Axis 2: Abstraction
*How close to raw problem material versus how close to thinking about thinking?*

This axis runs from ground level to meta level.

**Ground level** — the skill works directly on the problem's raw material: the facts, the possibilities, the evidence, the positions. It is close to the thing itself.

**Mid level** — the skill works on conclusions, structures, or framings derived from the raw material. It is one step removed from the thing itself.

**High level** — the skill works on the outputs of other thinking. It synthesizes, integrates, or stress-tests conclusions rather than engaging with raw material directly.

**Meta level** — the skill works on the thinking itself rather than on any problem content. It directs, maps, or monitors the reasoning process.

### Axis 3: Certainty
*What is the skill's relationship with uncertainty — in its inputs and in its outputs?*

This axis has two components that travel together but are distinct:

**Input tolerance** — how much uncertainty can the skill work with productively? Some skills need firm material to function. Others are specifically designed for uncertain or ambiguous conditions.

**Output character** — what kind of certainty does the skill produce? Some skills produce possibilities (low certainty output). Some produce tested conclusions (higher certainty output). Some produce understanding that is neither certain nor uncertain but integrative.

These two components are described together for each skill because they reveal the skill's natural position in a sequence: a skill with high input tolerance and low output certainty belongs early in a chain. A skill with low input tolerance and high output certainty belongs later, after earlier skills have firmed up the material.

## The Skills Mapped

### Conductor
- **Time:** Throughout — present at every stage, never absent
- **Abstraction:** Meta — works on the thinking process itself, not on problem content
- **Input tolerance:** High — can activate on any state of the thinking, certain or uncertain
- **Output character:** Directive — produces routing decisions, not conclusions about the problem

### Skill-master
- **Time:** Throughout — the baton is always present
- **Abstraction:** Meta — shapes how any skill engages with its material, not what the material is
- **Input tolerance:** High — applicable at any stage
- **Output character:** Orientating — produces a configured cognitive stance, not a conclusion

*Note: Conductor and skill-master are the only skills that sit outside the time axis. They are the frame around the picture, not content within it.*

---

### Discrepancies
- **Time:** Early — the earliest activating skill; upstream of framing
- **Abstraction:** Mid — works on the structure of a situation, not raw facts and not yet conclusions
- **Input tolerance:** Maximum — specifically designed for conditions where something feels wrong without knowing what; the less clear the situation, the more appropriate this skill is
- **Output character:** Signal, low certainty — produces named anomalies and assessed signal strength, not conclusions; hands off to other skills for diagnosis

### Reframe
- **Time:** Early — activates before analysis, when the frame itself is the problem
- **Abstraction:** Mid-high — works on the frame around the problem rather than the problem's content
- **Input tolerance:** High — activates specifically in stuck or ambiguous conditions
- **Output character:** Structural, medium certainty — produces a deliberately chosen alternative frame; more certain than raw signal but not yet a conclusion about the problem's content

### Decompose
- **Time:** Early-to-middle — activates after a problem exists but before analysis begins; sometimes preceded by reframe
- **Abstraction:** Mid — works on problem structure directly; closer to ground level than reframe
- **Input tolerance:** High for complexity, low for clarity — works best on tangled problems; unnecessary on clear ones
- **Output character:** Structural, medium-high certainty — produces named components with mapped dependencies; more certain than reframe output because the components are concrete

---

### Speculate
- **Time:** Middle — activates once the problem is correctly framed and clearly understood
- **Abstraction:** Ground — works directly on problem material, generating possibilities from it
- **Input tolerance:** Medium — needs a clear frame but not a firm conclusion; works in generative uncertainty
- **Output character:** Possibilities, low certainty — explicitly does not commit to conclusions; high volume, low individual certainty

### Infer
- **Time:** Middle — activates anywhere evidence exists but certainty does not; can appear early or late depending on where gaps appear
- **Abstraction:** Ground-to-mid — works on evidence and what it suggests; one step above raw facts
- **Input tolerance:** High for incompleteness — specifically designed for partial evidence; lower tolerance for complete absence of evidence
- **Output character:** Calibrated inference, variable certainty — explicitly labels the confidence level of its output; uniquely honest about its own certainty character

### Steelman
- **Time:** Middle — activates before engaging with an opposing position; follows discrepancies or reframe if the opposition revealed something unexpected
- **Abstraction:** Ground-to-mid — works on the content of an opposing position directly
- **Input tolerance:** Medium — needs an actual position to work with; not useful in pure uncertainty
- **Output character:** Reconstructed position, medium-high certainty — Stage 1 produces the strongest logical case; Stage 2 produces genuine understanding of why it is held; combined output is high fidelity even if the position itself remains disagreed with

---

### Reconsider
- **Time:** Middle-to-late — activates after a conclusion exists; tests it from the inside
- **Abstraction:** Mid — works on conclusions derived from problem material; one step removed from ground
- **Input tolerance:** Low-to-medium — needs a specific conclusion to test; cannot operate on pure uncertainty
- **Output character:** Tested conclusion, higher certainty — produces either increased confidence or a modified conclusion; raises the certainty floor of whatever it processes

### Challenge
- **Time:** Middle-to-late — activates after reconsider or after a position is ready for external pressure
- **Abstraction:** Mid — works on positions and conclusions; adjacent to reconsider on the abstraction axis
- **Input tolerance:** Low — needs a firm position; challenge applied to an uncertain position produces noise rather than pressure
- **Output character:** Stress-tested conclusion, high certainty — what survives challenge is genuinely solid; what does not survive reveals real weakness

### Constrain
- **Time:** Late-middle — activates after a conclusion or principle exists and needs to be made precise
- **Abstraction:** Mid — works on claims and principles; close to challenge and reconsider in abstraction space
- **Input tolerance:** Low-to-medium — needs something concrete to find the boundaries of
- **Output character:** Bounded claim, high certainty within domain — produces precision; the output is more certain than the input because it now has explicit limits

---

### Review
- **Time:** Late — activates after multiple skills have been used; synthesizes across outputs
- **Abstraction:** High — works on the outputs of other thinking, not on raw material
- **Input tolerance:** High for multiplicity — needs multiple perspectives to synthesize; low tolerance for single-perspective input (nothing to synthesize)
- **Output character:** Integrative understanding, emergent certainty — produces something that no single perspective alone would have produced; certainty character is neither high nor low but integrative — it holds tensions rather than resolving them

### Pre-mortem
- **Time:** Late — activates when a commitment is imminent; the final stress-test before action
- **Abstraction:** High — works on a decision and its potential futures, not on the problem's raw material
- **Input tolerance:** Low — needs a specific, nearly-made decision; not useful in early stages
- **Output character:** Concrete failure stories and preventable actions, actionable certainty — produces specific, usable outputs rather than abstract conclusions

## The Natural Clusters

Skills that are close in all three dimensions are natural tandems — they can be activated simultaneously or in rapid alternation without the transition cost of moving across the topology.

### Cluster 1: The Framing Cluster
*discrepancies, reframe, decompose*

All three are early in time, mid in abstraction, and high in input tolerance. They work on problem structure before analysis begins. Natural sequence within the cluster: discrepancies detects that something is wrong → reframe addresses the frame if that is the problem → decompose breaks the correctly-framed problem into workable units. Can also be activated in parallel when a problem is both wrongly framed and tangled.

### Cluster 2: The Generation Cluster
*speculate, infer, steelman*

All three are middle in time, ground-to-mid in abstraction, and produce low-to-medium certainty output. They work on problem material directly, generating content for the testing cluster to work on. Natural tandems within this cluster: speculate + infer (generate possibilities, assess which are most likely given evidence); steelman + speculate (generate the strongest opposing position, then speculate on responses to it).

### Cluster 3: The Testing Cluster
*reconsider, challenge, constrain*

All three are middle-to-late in time, mid in abstraction, and low in input tolerance — they need firm material. Natural sequence within the cluster: reconsider tests from the inside → challenge tests from the outside → constrain makes the surviving conclusion precise. Can run in that sequence or in pairs depending on what the problem needs.

### Cluster 4: The Synthesis Cluster
*review, pre-mortem*

Both are late in time, high in abstraction. Review synthesizes understanding. Pre-mortem stress-tests commitment. Natural sequence: review produces integrated understanding → pre-mortem stress-tests the commitment that understanding supports. Or: pre-mortem reveals concerns → review synthesizes those concerns with existing understanding to produce a revised position.

### Cluster 5: The Meta Layer
*conductor, skill-master*

Both are throughout in time, meta in abstraction. Not tandems with each other — they have a defined relationship: conductor directs, skill-master is the instrument of direction. Their natural coupling is structural rather than situational.

## The Natural Edges

Beyond clusters, certain cross-cluster edges are strong — pairs of skills that frequently connect across cluster boundaries.

**discrepancies → pre-mortem:** Detected anomalies in an imminent commitment route directly to pre-mortem without needing to traverse the full topology.

**steelman → challenge:** The most common cross-cluster tandem in the system. Steelman constructs the strongest opposing position. Challenge tests your position against it. Together they form a complete engagement loop.

**reframe → speculate:** A new frame opens a new solution space. Speculate is almost always the right next move after a successful reframe.

**infer → reconsider:** An inference with load-bearing gaps almost always needs reconsider to test the assumption structure underneath it.

**review → reframe:** Sometimes synthesis reveals the frame was wrong all along. Review is one of the few late-stage skills that can send thinking all the way back to the framing cluster.

**decompose → [any testing skill]:** Each named component from decompose becomes a workable unit that can be handed directly to reconsider, challenge, or constrain depending on what the component needs.

## Coupling Patterns Derived From the Topology

These are not rules. They are patterns that emerge from the shape of the space. The conductor uses them as starting points, not conclusions.

### Tandem activation
*When two skills are in the same cluster and the problem benefits from holding both simultaneously.*

Natural tandems: speculate + constrain (generate and bound simultaneously, producing a rhythm); steelman + challenge (construct and test simultaneously, holding the tension); reconsider + infer (test a conclusion while remaining explicit about what is still uncertain).

The integration point for any tandem is review — the synthesis cluster is where simultaneously held tensions are resolved into understanding.

### Layered activation
*When the problem benefits from being processed at multiple abstraction levels sequentially, where each layer's output becomes the next layer's input.*

Natural layering: ground layer first (speculate, infer, steelman — work on raw material), mid layer second (reconsider, challenge, constrain — work on conclusions derived from that material), high layer third (review — synthesize the outputs of both layers). Each layer is complete before the next begins.

This pattern keeps abstraction levels from contaminating each other — values don't shape what counts as evidence, pragmatic constraints don't shape what counts as true.

### Intentional exclusion
*When a lens or skill is deliberately suspended for a phase of thinking, then reintroduced.*

The conductor tracks the exclusion and ensures reintroduction. Common exclusions: suspending steelman's empathic stage temporarily to get clean logical analysis before reintroducing the human dimension; suspending pragmatic considerations during the generation cluster to see what is true before asking what is actionable; suspending challenge during speculate to protect the generative phase from premature pressure.

Exclusion is only useful when named. Unnamed exclusion is bias. Named exclusion is a tool.

### Recursive re-entry
*When downstream output reveals an upstream problem, sending thinking back to an earlier cluster.*

The topology makes re-entry legible: if a testing-cluster skill produces output that questions the frame, the conductor routes back to the framing cluster. If a synthesis-cluster skill produces output that reveals an unconsidered angle, the conductor routes back to the generation cluster. The direction of re-entry is always upstream — toward earlier time, lower abstraction, higher input tolerance.

## How to Use This Skill

Topology is consulted, not executed. It does not produce a conclusion about the problem. It returns a position and a description of what is nearby.

**When the conductor is uncertain about routing:** locate the current state of thinking on the three axes. Read what cluster it is in. Read the natural next moves from that cluster. Return that information to the conductor.

**When coupling is being considered:** locate the skills being considered on the three axes. Are they in the same cluster? Are they connected by a natural edge? Read the appropriate coupling pattern. Return that to the conductor.

**When the system seems to be cycling without progress:** locate where the thinking has been on the time axis. If it has not reached the synthesis cluster, it is not yet complete. If it has reached synthesis and is cycling back, check whether the re-entry was warranted or whether the system is avoiding commitment.

---

## Updating the Topology

When a new skill is added to the system, the topology must be updated with that skill's position on all three axes, its cluster membership or isolation, and its natural edges.

A skill that exists outside the topology is a skill whose coupling properties are unknown. The system can still use it — but the conductor cannot reason about how it relates to other skills, which means coupling decisions involving it will be intuitive rather than derived from the shape.

The topology is the system's self-knowledge of its own structure. It should be as complete as the system itself.
