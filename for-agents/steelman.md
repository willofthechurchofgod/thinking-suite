# STEELMAN: Constructing the Strongest Version of a Position You Disagree With

## What This Skill Is

STEELMAN is a configuration for genuinely understanding a position before engaging with it. Not the weakest version you can knock down — the strongest version a thoughtful defender would actually hold.

It runs in two stages because there are two distinct ways a steelman can fail: you can win the logical argument while completely missing why the position matters to the person holding it. Both stages are required for genuine understanding.

---

## The Configuration

**LENS:** charitable:reconstruction + empathy:perspective-taking
**DEPTH:** 70-80%
**LIGHT:** 80% (bright enough to see what the position is actually responding to)

---

## Stage 1: Logical Steelman

### What it produces
The strongest rational case for the position. Evidence, reasoning, and values a thoughtful defender would actually cite — not a caricature, not a strawman, the real argument at full strength.

### How to run it

**Step 1: Suspend your objections**
Not permanently. Temporarily. You cannot reconstruct a position you are simultaneously arguing against. Set your objections aside for the duration of Stage 1.

**Step 2: Identify the core claim**
What is the position actually asserting? State it precisely, in terms its defender would recognize.
Not your paraphrase. Their claim.

**Step 3: Find the strongest evidence**
What evidence would a thoughtful defender cite? Not cherry-picked or weak evidence — the best available evidence that genuinely supports the position.

**Step 4: Find the strongest reasoning**
Given that evidence, what is the most coherent logical path to the conclusion?
What assumptions does the position rest on, and are those assumptions defensible?

**Step 5: Find the values underneath**
Every position rests on values, not just facts. What values — prioritized in what order — would lead a reasonable person to this conclusion?
Name them explicitly. This is where many steelmans fail: they reconstruct the logic but miss the value structure underneath it.

**Step 6: State the steelman**
Articulate the position as its strongest defender would. If you showed this to a thoughtful proponent, would they say "yes, that's my argument, stated better than I stated it"?
That is the test. If the answer is no, you have not yet completed Stage 1.

---

## Stage 2: Empathic Steelman

### What it produces
Understanding of why a reasonable person holds this position genuinely — what experience, context, or concern makes it feel not just defensible but important.

### Why this stage is necessary
You can complete Stage 1 and still be condescending. Logical steelman reconstructs the argument. Empathic steelman reconstructs the person making it. Both are required for genuine engagement.

Winning an argument while completely missing why it was being made is one of the most common and least noticed failures in reasoning.

### How to run it

**Step 1: Ask what the position is responding to**
Every position is a response to something — an experience, a fear, an observation, a pattern someone has seen. What is this position responding to?
Not abstractly. Concretely. What would someone have had to see or experience to arrive here?

**Step 2: Find the legitimate concern**
Underneath most positions that seem wrong is a legitimate concern that the position is (perhaps poorly) trying to address. What is the legitimate concern here?
Name it without sarcasm. If you cannot name it without sarcasm, you have not found it yet.

**Step 3: Understand the stakes**
Why does this matter to the person holding it? What is at stake for them if they are wrong, or if the opposite position wins?
Stakes explain intensity. A position held with unusual conviction is usually protecting something real.

**Step 4: Reconstruct the lived logic**
Given the concern, the stakes, and the experience that produced this view — what is the internal logic that makes this position feel not just acceptable but necessary?
Not "why are they wrong." Why does it feel right from inside.

**Step 5: State the empathic steelman**
Articulate what the position means to its holder. If you showed this to someone who holds the view, would they say "yes, you actually understand why I believe this"?
That is the test. Not agreement — understanding.

---

## The Integration of Both Stages

Stage 1 and Stage 2 are sequential, not parallel.

Logical steelman first — because you need to understand what is being claimed before you can understand why it is held.

Empathic steelman second — because understanding why it is held often reveals dimensions of the claim that the logical reconstruction missed.

After both stages: you now have a complete steelman. You understand the argument at its strongest and the person making it at their most genuine.

Only now should you engage with challenge, reconsider, or constrain.

## Connections to Other Skills

**STEELMAN → CHALLENGE:** You cannot effectively challenge a position you have not genuinely understood. Run steelman first, then challenge the strongest version.

**STEELMAN → REVIEW:** When you have steelmanned multiple competing positions, use review to find what each position is seeing that the others miss.

**STEELMAN → CONSTRAIN:** Once you understand a position at full strength, use constrain to find where it applies and where it breaks.

**SKILL MASTER Q4 → STEELMAN:** When you notice you are locked into your own perspective and cannot see the opposing view clearly, steelman is the rotation tool.

## Failure Modes

**The logical-only steelman:** You complete Stage 1 but skip Stage 2. You now have the best argument but not the person. You will engage with the argument and miss the conversation.
Recovery: Stage 2 is not optional. Run it.

**The charitable-sounding strawman:** Your steelman sounds generous but subtly weakens the position — you've included the concessions the defender would make while omitting the strongest parts.
Detection: Would the position's defender recognize your steelman as stronger than their own statement of it?
Recovery: Return to Step 6 of Stage 1. Apply the test honestly.

**Empathy as condescension:** Stage 2 produces "I understand why someone with their background would believe this" — which is understanding the position as a symptom rather than as a view.
Recovery: The empathic steelman is not explaining why they are wrong in an understandable way. It is explaining why they are not obviously wrong from inside their own framework.

**Premature engagement:** You begin Stage 1 and your objections keep surfacing, pulling you toward rebuttal before the steelman is complete.
Recovery: Objections are useful. Write them down. Return to them after both stages are done. They will be sharper after the steelman anyway.

---

## Success Criteria

STEELMAN worked when:
1. A thoughtful proponent would recognize Stage 1 as the strongest version of their argument
2. A genuine holder of the position would recognize Stage 2 as understanding why they hold it
3. You discovered something about the position you did not know before running the skill
4. Your subsequent engagement (via challenge or otherwise) addresses the real argument, not a weakened version

STEELMAN failed when:
1. Your steelman is something the position's holder would find condescending or incomplete
2. You completed Stage 1 but skipped Stage 2 and called it done
3. You emerged with the same objections, unchanged, as when you started
4. The steelman was performed rather than genuinely constructed
