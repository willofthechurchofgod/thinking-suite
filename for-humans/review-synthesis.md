# Review: Synthesizing Multiple Perspectives

## What This Is

Review is for holding multiple perspectives, conclusions, or lines of thinking simultaneously and asking: what emerges when I see all of these at once?

This is synthesis — moving from "here are several possibilities" to "here is what actually matters." It's the skill that makes the others add up to more than the sum of their parts.

---

## When to Use It

Use review when:
- You've explored a problem from multiple angles using other skills
- You need to move from analysis to synthesis
- You have contradictory conclusions that need to be held together rather than resolved
- You're looking for patterns that no single perspective alone would show

Don't use review when you have only one perspective (there's nothing to synthesize), when you need to choose between options (use challenge or constrain), or before doing the underlying exploration.

---

## How to Do It

### Step 1: Assemble the perspectives

Gather all the different ways you've looked at the problem:
- Different approaches you used (speculate, reconsider, infer, etc.)
- Different lenses you applied (logic, values, pragmatism, emotion)
- Different conclusions you reached
- Different objections you considered

### Step 2: Hold them simultaneously

This is the hard part. You're not choosing between them. You're holding them all at once.

Resist the urge to resolve tension or pick one. The tension is information.

### Step 3: Look for contradictions

Where do the perspectives fundamentally disagree? Why do they disagree?

Understanding *why* two perspectives produce different conclusions reveals something neither would show alone.

### Step 4: Look for patterns

What themes appear across multiple perspectives? What do all perspectives have in common? What do only some share?

### Step 5: Ask the synthesis question

Not: "Which is right?"
But: "What pattern emerges? What do I understand now that I didn't understand before?"

### Step 6: Name the emergent insight

What new understanding arose from holding multiple perspectives? This is what no single perspective alone would have produced.

---

## The Mechanics

**Perspective inventory:** List every perspective taken. Be explicit about each view and what it showed.

**Contradiction identification:** Where do these perspectives contradict? Why? Understanding why two perspectives disagree reveals something neither would show alone.

**Pattern recognition:** What appears across multiple perspectives? What is consistent? What varies?

**Contradiction resolution:** You don't resolve contradictions by choosing one side. You resolve them by understanding why both seem true. Both can be valid — in different contexts, at different levels, with different weights.

**Synthesis:** What is the new understanding that came from seeing all perspectives together? This is not something any single perspective revealed. It emerged from the combination.

---

## Variations

**Multi-approach review:** Synthesize results from using multiple skills on the same problem. Focus on what each approach contributed and what none of them would have shown alone.

**Multi-lens review:** Synthesize what emerges when looking at the same problem through multiple lenses — logic, values, pragmatism, emotion. Often reveals that apparent disagreements are actually perspectives on different aspects of the same thing.

**Contradiction review:** When two perspectives directly contradict, hold the tension and ask: "Why would intelligent people reach both conclusions? What is each one seeing?" The answer is usually more interesting than either conclusion alone.

**Evolution review:** Synthesize how your thinking has evolved. "Here is what I thought before. Here is what I think now. What changed, and why?"

---

## Connections to Other Skills

Review is the synthesis skill — it comes after multiple other skills have been used.

**Full cycle:** speculate → reconsider → constrain → challenge → review

**Multiple angles:** speculate (angle 1) → speculate (angle 2) → speculate (angle 3) → review

**Inference pattern:** infer → infer → infer → review (find pattern across multiple inferences)

---

## Failure Modes

**Forcing resolution too early:** You hold contradictory perspectives for a moment and quickly choose one. Recovery: hold the contradiction longer. Ask "Why would intelligent people reach both conclusions?"

**Missing the real pattern:** You see surface patterns but miss the deeper one. Recovery: ask "If I layer these perspectives differently, what becomes visible?"

**Performing synthesis:** You go through the steps without discovering anything new. Detection: you emerge with the same conclusion as before. Recovery: slow down. If nothing changed, you missed something.

**Over-complexity:** You synthesize so much the result is more complex than useful. Recovery: ask "What is the simplest way to state what I've discovered?"

**Losing original perspectives:** Your synthesis no longer respects what each perspective actually showed. Recovery: frequently return to the originals and verify consistency.

---

## Success Criteria

This worked when:
1. You held multiple perspectives without immediately resolving them
2. You discovered contradictions between perspectives
3. You understood why the contradictions exist
4. You found patterns that spanned multiple perspectives
5. You arrived at understanding that no single perspective alone would have produced
6. You can explain what each perspective contributed

This failed when:
1. You resolved contradictions too quickly
2. You ended with the same understanding as before
3. You couldn't explain why holding multiple perspectives was valuable

---

## A Note on Using This Skill on Itself

Review is also what you should do with the entire skill set.

Holding speculate, reconsider, infer, constrain, challenge, and review together and asking: "What do these skills together teach me about how to think well?" — that is review working at the meta level. You might discover that some work better together than others, or that the order matters, or that certain combinations are more powerful than expected.

That discovery is the system becoming more than the sum of its parts.
