# How the Skills Relate

## What This Is

The topology is a map of the thinking system in three dimensions. It tells you which skills are natural partners, which tend to follow each other, and why. Use it when you're not sure what comes next, or when you're considering using two skills at once.

---

## The Three Dimensions

### When in the process?

Skills have a natural moment where they're most useful:

**Early** — before you understand the problem. These skills work on the problem structure itself, before any analysis of content.

**Middle** — once the problem is clear and you're generating or testing ideas. These work on problem content directly.

**Late** — once substantial thinking is done and you're integrating. These work on the outputs of other thinking.

**Throughout** — always present. These direct and shape thinking at any stage, not just one.

### How abstract?

**Ground level:** the skill works directly on raw material — facts, possibilities, evidence, positions.

**Mid level:** works on conclusions and structures derived from raw material. One step removed.

**High level:** works on the outputs of other thinking — synthesizing, integrating, stress-testing.

**Meta level:** works on the thinking process itself rather than on any problem content.

### Relationship with uncertainty?

**Input tolerance:** how much uncertainty can the skill work with productively?

**Output character:** what kind of certainty does the skill produce?

A skill that accepts high uncertainty as input and produces low-certainty output (possibilities) belongs early in a sequence. A skill that needs firm input and produces high-certainty output (tested conclusions) belongs later.

---

## Where Each Skill Sits

**conductor / skill-master**
Throughout · Meta · Works at any stage on the thinking process itself

**discrepancies**
Early · Mid · Maximum input tolerance · Produces named signals (low certainty output)

**reframe**
Early · Mid-high · High input tolerance · Produces alternative frames (medium certainty)

**decompose**
Early-middle · Mid · Works on tangled problems · Produces named components (medium-high certainty)

**speculate**
Middle · Ground · Needs a clear frame · Produces possibilities (low certainty output)

**infer**
Middle · Ground-to-mid · High tolerance for incomplete information · Produces calibrated guesses (variable certainty, explicitly labeled)

**steelman**
Middle · Ground-to-mid · Needs a real position to work with · Produces reconstructed understanding (medium-high certainty)

**reconsider**
Middle-to-late · Mid · Needs a specific conclusion · Produces tested conclusions (higher certainty)

**challenge**
Middle-to-late · Mid · Needs a firm position · Produces stress-tested conclusions (high certainty)

**constrain**
Late-middle · Mid · Needs something concrete to find the limits of · Produces bounded claims (high certainty within domain)

**review**
Late · High · Needs multiple perspectives to synthesize · Produces emergent understanding (integrative)

**pre-mortem**
Late · High · Needs a specific near-made decision · Produces concrete failure stories (actionable)

## The Natural Clusters

Skills close to each other on all three dimensions can be used together naturally — switching between them is low-cost, and holding both at once is tractable.

**Framing Cluster:** *discrepancies, reframe, decompose*
All early, all high input tolerance. Natural sequence: discrepancies finds something wrong → reframe addresses the frame if that's the problem → decompose breaks the correctly-framed problem into parts.

**Generation Cluster:** *speculate, infer, steelman*
All middle, all ground-level, all produce lower-certainty output. Natural pairs: speculate + infer (generate possibilities, then assess which are most likely); steelman + speculate (build the strongest opposing case, then generate responses).

**Testing Cluster:** *reconsider, challenge, constrain*
All middle-to-late, all need firm material. Natural sequence: reconsider tests from the inside → challenge tests from the outside → constrain makes the surviving claim precise.

**Synthesis Cluster:** *review, pre-mortem*
Both late, both high abstraction. Natural sequence: review integrates understanding → pre-mortem stress-tests the commitment that understanding supports.

---

## Strong Cross-Cluster Connections

**discrepancies → pre-mortem:** If something feels wrong and you're about to commit, skip straight to pre-mortem without traversing the full sequence.

**steelman → challenge:** The most common pairing. Build the strongest opposing position, then test your own against it.

**reframe → speculate:** A new frame opens a new possibility space. Speculate is almost always the right next step after a successful reframe.

**infer → reconsider:** An inference with important gaps almost always needs reconsider to test its assumptions.

**review → reframe:** Sometimes synthesis reveals the frame was wrong all along. Review is one of the few late-stage skills that can send you all the way back to the beginning.

## How to Use This

You don't need to consult this map constantly. Use it when:

- You're not sure what to do next after a skill finishes
- You're considering using two skills at once and want to know if they're compatible
- Your thinking seems to be cycling without progress — check where you are on the time dimension. If you haven't reached synthesis, you're not done yet.

**Natural ways to use skills together:**

*Tandem:* Two skills from the same cluster, held simultaneously — generate and bound at the same time (*speculate + constrain*), build and test at the same time (*steelman + challenge*). The integration point is almost always *review*.

*Layered:* Process the same material at multiple abstraction levels in sequence — ground level first (speculate, infer, steelman), mid level second (reconsider, challenge, constrain), high level third (review). Each layer's output becomes the next layer's input.

*Intentional exclusion:* Deliberately suspend one lens for a phase, then reintroduce it. For example: suspend evaluation during speculate to protect the generative phase. Name the exclusion — unnamed exclusion is bias, named exclusion is a tool.

*Re-entry:* When downstream output reveals an upstream problem, go back. Testing reveals the frame was wrong → return to framing. Synthesis reveals an unconsidered angle → return to generation. Always move upstream: earlier in time, closer to ground level.
