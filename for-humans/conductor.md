# How to Move Between Skills

## What This Is

The conductor is the part of your thinking that watches the whole process — not the thinking about the problem, but the thinking about the thinking. It decides which skill to use, when to switch, and when you're actually done.

Most people have a favorite cognitive tool. They find it and use it for everything. The conductor is the habit of noticing when your current approach isn't working and choosing a different one.

---

## The Terrain

The skills in this system aren't random. They sit in layers that reflect the natural progression of thinking:

**Detection:** Something feels off but you don't know what yet.
→ Use *discrepancies*

**Framing:** You're not sure you're solving the right problem.
→ Use *reframe* or *decompose*

**Generation:** The problem is clear but unexplored.
→ Use *speculate*, *infer*, or *steelman*

**Testing:** You have a position that needs pressure.
→ Use *reconsider*, *challenge*, or *constrain*

**Synthesis:** You've explored enough and need to integrate.
→ Use *review* or *pre-mortem*

These aren't strict rules. They're the natural flow. Most thinking that fails does so because it skips layers — jumping to testing before framing is right, or generating without ever testing.

---

## How to Start

When you first encounter a problem, run the five questions from *skill-master*. Then locate yourself:

- Something feels wrong without a clear reason → **discrepancies** first
- The problem itself seems unclear or tangled → **reframe** or **decompose**
- Problem is clear but you don't have a position yet → **speculate** or **infer**
- You already have a position but want to test it → **reconsider** or **challenge**
- You've done a lot of thinking and need to integrate it → **review**
- You're about to commit to something significant → **pre-mortem**

If none of these feel right, start with *discrepancies*. The detection layer is the right default when you're not sure where you are.

---

## How to Keep Moving

After each skill produces output, ask three things:

**1. Is this complete?** Did the skill do what it's for, or is there more to do here?

**2. What does this output suggest?** Each skill's output points toward natural next steps. Follow those signals rather than mechanically moving to the next skill on a list.

**3. Has the terrain shifted?** Sometimes what you discover during testing reveals that your frame was wrong — or what you discover during synthesis reveals you missed an angle. When this happens, go back upstream. That's not failure; that's the process working.

---

## How to Know You're Done

Completion is not exhaustion. You don't need to use every skill. You need to use the right skills until you have understanding adequate to the problem.

**Signs that thinking is complete:**
- You've synthesized multiple perspectives and understand the problem better than before
- You could explain your position, its assumptions, and its limits
- Further thinking is producing variations rather than new insight

**Signs that thinking feels done but isn't:**
- The conclusion arrived without any resistance
- It perfectly matches what you hoped for
- Nothing along the way required you to update a prior assumption
- You haven't reached the synthesis layer

When you're genuinely done, do a final pass: is the reasoning honest (logic)? are you naming things accurately (grammar)? is the communication serving truth rather than acceptance (rhetoric)?

---

## When You're Uncertain About Which Skill to Use

Check the *topology* — it maps how skills relate to each other and which tend to work together. Skills that are close in the topology (similar in timing, abstraction level, and relationship to uncertainty) are natural pairs. Skills that are far apart work better sequentially, with *review* as the integration point.

If you're uncertain about the terrain itself rather than just the routing:
- Run *discrepancies* on your current thinking, not just on the original problem
- Or run *decompose* on your thinking process — what parts of the problem have you addressed and what have you not?

---

## What the Conductor Is Not

It's not a rule that every skill must be used on every problem. Most problems need only a few.

It's not a replacement for judgment. The map and the terrain description exist to help — the judgment about what the problem actually needs still requires honest engagement.

It's not infallible. If your routing feels like performance rather than genuine navigation, return to *discrepancies* and check the routing itself.
