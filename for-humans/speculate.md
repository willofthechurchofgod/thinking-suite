# Speculate: Generating Possibilities

## What This Is

Speculate is for generating many possibilities without committing to any of them. Think in the subjunctive mood — "what if?" rather than "this is."

The discipline is to generate first, evaluate later. Every other skill asks you to think carefully and precisely. Speculate asks you to think freely and prolifically. Volume is the goal. Novelty is the prize.

---

## When to Use It

Use speculate when:
- You need to explore a solution space before choosing a direction
- You're early in a problem and don't yet have a position
- You want to see what becomes possible if you loosen a constraint
- You've been thinking in one direction for a while and want to check whether you've missed alternatives

Don't use speculate when a decision needs to be made right now, when you need precision from the start, or when you're already committed to a direction. Speculate is for before commitment, not during or after.

---

## How to Do It

### Step 1: Frame the question specifically

Not: "How could we improve things?"
Better: "What would a system that prioritizes user autonomy over efficiency look like?"

Specificity in the question produces specificity in the answers.

### Step 2: Loosen constraints

Ask: "What if we removed constraint X?" "What if we had unlimited resources?" "What if we flipped the priority?"

You're not ignoring real constraints forever — you're temporarily setting them aside to see what's visible without them.

### Step 3: Generate without evaluating

Don't pause to evaluate each idea. Generate at least 10 substantive ideas. Evaluation comes later.

When an idea feels impractical, write it down anyway and keep going. The impractical idea often contains the seed of the practical one.

### Step 4: Look for surprising ideas

Some ideas will be obvious variations. Some will be genuinely surprising. The surprising ones are valuable even if not practical — they show you that the solution space is larger than you assumed.

### Step 5: Notice patterns

Step back and look at the ideas together:
- What themes appear across multiple ideas?
- What novel combinations emerge?
- What assumptions did your ideas challenge?
- What surprised you?

---

## Variations

**Possibility expansion:** See how many different approaches could work. Generate volume, don't filter.

**Constraint relaxation:** Remove a specific constraint and see what becomes possible. "What would this be if cost were not a factor?"

**Value reorientation:** Prioritize a different value and see what changes. "What if we optimized for resilience instead of efficiency?"

**Wild speculation:** Generate ideas barely connected to current reality. Useful when you're very stuck or when the goal is to break out of a familiar pattern entirely.

---

## Connections to Other Skills

- **speculate → constrain:** Use constrain to explore where each promising idea breaks down
- **speculate → challenge:** Use challenge to test the solidity of the most promising ideas
- **speculate → reconsider:** Examine promising ideas from new angles
- **speculate → review:** Find patterns across multiple rounds of speculation

---

## Failure Modes

**Premature evaluation:** You generate ideas and immediately start evaluating them. Recovery: tell yourself "evaluation later." Generate first.

**Staying in familiar space:** You generate variations without pushing into genuinely novel territory. Recovery: ask "What would genuinely different look like?" Push harder.

**Losing the frame:** You start speculating about something different from what you intended. Recovery: return to the original question.

**Over-specification:** Getting caught in details instead of exploring breadth. Recovery: keep things sketchy. You're mapping the space, not building anything yet.

---

## Success Criteria

This worked when:
1. You generated at least 10 substantive ideas
2. At least 2-3 were genuinely surprising to you
3. You noticed patterns across the ideas
4. You identified at least one idea worth pursuing further
5. You didn't get stuck in evaluation or defending ideas

This failed when:
1. All ideas are minor variations on a theme
2. None of the ideas surprised you
3. You spent more time evaluating than generating
